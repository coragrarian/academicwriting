# The Discussion section

## The Discussion section of research papers

The exercises that follow will help you explore how authors interpret findings, relate results to previous research, propose tentative explanations, highlight contributions, and express different degrees of likelihood in the Discussion section of research papers.

### Exercise 1

In the Discussion section, writers address different questions as they interpret and discuss their findings.

**Instructions:** Match each question (1–4) with the excerpt (A–D) that best answers it.

| Excerpt | Text |
| --- | --- |
| A | In the present study, both low and moderate N additions did not significantly alter the *Q*<sub>10-soil</sub> value, which is consistent with the findings of Mo et al. (2008) and Tu et al. (2013). |
| B | Although LWP is used as an indicator for plant water status, our results did not exhibit the expected trends. This may be due to the more delicate leaf and unnoticeable damage of surface tissue while conducting the measurement with the pressure bomb. |
| C | Young apricot trees subjected to two periods of water deficit only diminished photosynthetic rate at the end of the dry periods, showing also a fast recovery afterwards. These results suggest that stomata behavior is not a passive response to water deficit. |
| D | With regard to the limitations of the present study, the small sample size and the participation of only one group of migrants during the prototype evaluation phases should be acknowledged. |

**Purposes:**

- <mark class="correct-answer">C</mark> What do the results mean? What can we conclude from them?
- <mark class="correct-answer">D</mark> What are the limitations of the study? What problems or weaknesses affected the study?
- <mark class="correct-answer">A</mark> How do the results compare with previous studies? Are the results similar to or different from what other studies found?
- <mark class="correct-answer">B</mark> What may have caused this result? What is a possible explanation for the result?
