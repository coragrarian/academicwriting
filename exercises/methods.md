# The Methods section

## Purpose of the Methods section

The exercises that follow will help you reflect on the purpose of the Methods section.

### Exercise 1

**Instructions:** Based on your own knowledge about research articles, what is the main purpose of the Methods section? Choose the option that best summarises it.

- [ ] a) To present the researcher’s opinions, interpretations, and personal reflections on the importance of the research topic.
- [x] b) To explain how the research was carried out and provide enough detail for others to replicate the study.
- [ ] c) To interpret the main findings and compare them with related studies reported in the literature.
- [ ] d) To summarise the study’s conclusions, discuss implications, and suggest possible directions for future research.

<!-- agrarian
feedback:
  options:
    a: >
      This describes interpretation and reflection rather than the main
      function of the Methods section.
    b: >
      Correct. The Methods section explains how the study was carried out

### Exercise 2

In academic writing, grammatical alternatives can differ in contextual appropriateness, meaning, or reporting emphasis.

The sentences below are project-authored examples.

**Instructions:** Which alternative is **least appropriate in this context** as a replacement for the word in **bold**? Consider the intended relationship: collecting samples, referring to a method reported by previous authors, or directly presenting information in a table.

#### Item A

*Soil samples were **taken** from the top 20 cm of the selected rice fields.*

- [ ] collected
- [x] made
- [ ] obtained

<!-- agrarian
feedback:
  correct: >
    Correct. "Made" suggests producing or preparing the soil rather than
    collecting it from an existing field. "Collected" and "obtained" fit
    the sampling relationship described here.
  incorrect: >
    Review the sampling context. The soil comes from the surface layer of
    rice fields; it is obtained or collected, rather than produced.
-->

#### Item B

*The seed mixtures were heated for one hour using a procedure **described** in an earlier study.*

- [x] assumed
- [ ] outlined
- [ ] proposed

<!-- agrarian
feedback:
  correct: >
    Correct. "Assumed" does not fit the intended relationship of referring
    to a method reported by earlier authors. "Outlined" is close to
    "described" here. "Proposed" is possible English, but suggests that
    the authors put the methodology forward, rather than merely described it.
  incorrect: >
    "Described" and "outlined" concern how a method is set out. "Proposed"
    changes the attribution to putting it forward. Neither is an exact
    synonym in every context, but "assumed" fits this reporting relationship
    least well.
-->

#### Item C

*The equations used in our analysis are **presented** in Table 1.*

- [ ] given
- [x] seen
- [ ] shown

<!-- agrarian
feedback:
  correct: >
    Correct. "Seen" is possible English, including in academic expressions
    such as "as can be seen in Table 1". For this direct statement of where
    equations are provided, "given" and "shown" are more conventional;
    "seen" places the emphasis on the reader's observation.
  incorrect: >
    Judge the alternatives as direct presentation of information in a table.
    "Given" and "shown" perform that function conventionally. "Seen" is
    grammatical, but is less conventional in this bare "are ... in Table 1"
    frame; it often appears in "as (can be) seen" instead.
-->

### Exercise 3

**Instructions:** For each item below, select the **two** alternatives that express the same meaning as the original sentence.

The sentences and study details below are project-authored illustrations.

#### Item A

**Original sentence:**

> *For this calculation, we assumed that temporary spontaneous cover crops (TSCC) occupied 100% of the soil surface in the selected olive groves.*

- [x] For this calculation, it was assumed that TSCC occupied 100% of the soil surface in the selected olive groves.
- [x] For this calculation, we considered TSCC to occupy 100% of the soil surface in the selected olive groves.
- [ ] For this calculation, we demonstrated that TSCC occupied 100% of the soil surface in the selected olive groves.

<!-- agrarian
feedback:
  correct: >
    Correct. The first option changes the sentence to the passive voice, and
    the second replaces "assumed" with a similar expression. Neither changes
    the original claim.
  incorrect: >
    "Demonstrated" expresses stronger evidential certainty than "assumed".
    Look for the two alternatives that preserve the original meaning.
-->

#### Item B

**Original sentence:**

> *Each treatment consisted of 21 trees arranged in three rows of seven; measurements used five trees from the central row.*

- [x] Each treatment comprised 21 trees in three rows of seven; five central-row trees were measured.
- [ ] Each treatment produced 21 trees in three rows of seven; five central-row trees were measured.
- [x] Each treatment included 21 trees in three rows of seven; five central-row trees were measured.

<!-- agrarian
feedback:
  correct: >
    Correct. "Comprised" and "included" preserve the idea that 21 trees made
    up each treatment.
  incorrect: >
    "Produced" changes the relationship expressed in the original sentence.
    The treatments contained 21 trees; they did not produce them.
-->
